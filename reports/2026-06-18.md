## 💡 IDEA 1 — PeakDate
> Tells a food blogger the exact last day to publish each seasonal recipe so it ranks on Google before the search spike — and nags them backward from that date.

**Who is this for?**
Priya, a solo food blogger / recipe developer who runs her site as her income (ads + sponsorships), publishing 4–8 recipes a month and living or dying by seasonal Google + Pinterest traffic.

**What problem does it solve?**
It's late September and Priya realizes her pumpkin soup recipe still isn't written — and she half-remembers "publish 8–12 weeks early," so she's probably already late and won't rank until next year. She has 40 recipe ideas in a spreadsheet and no idea which are about to "expire" for this season. She guesses, publishes the wrong thing, and watches a competitor's pumpkin post climb instead.

**Real world example:**
Meet Priya, a solo food blogger. Every season she manually cross-checks Google Trends, counts back weeks on a calendar, and guesses which recipes to write first. With PeakDate she opens a dashboard and sees "Publish 'Pumpkin Soup' by Sept 4 — 6 days left" sorted by urgency, and writes the right thing first in 2 minutes instead of an hour of anxious guessing.

**How it works:**
1. She imports her recipe idea list and tags each with its season/holiday.
2. PeakDate looks up the historical search-rise curve and subtracts Google's ranking-maturation lag plus Pinterest's 45–60 day pin runway.
3. It returns one ranked "publish-by" deadline list and emails her each Monday: "These 3 recipes must go out this week or you miss the season."

**Price:** $19/month — one missed seasonal recipe is a year of lost ad-RPM traffic, so a deadline that prevents that pays for itself.

## 💡 IDEA 2 — FirstMover
> Surfaces local businesses that just hit a "you now need my service" trigger — a new funding round, a new lease, a growth hire — and hands a freelancer a ranked daily prospect list.

**Who is this for?**
Aditya, a freelance brand/web designer (or fractional copywriter / videographer) selling $2,000–8,000 projects to small companies, who is good at the work but hates cold prospecting.

**What problem does it solve?**
It's a Tuesday and Aditya has no pipeline. He knows the best time to win a website project is right when a company raises seed money or opens a second location — but by the time he spots it on LinkedIn, three other freelancers have already pitched. He spends 90 minutes scrolling, finds nobody timely, and sends two stale cold emails to companies that redesigned six months ago.

**Real world example:**
Meet Aditya, a freelance web designer. Every week he loses hours hunting for companies that might need a redesign right now. With FirstMover he opens a Monday list of 12 local companies that raised money or opened a location in the last 14 days — exactly when they re-brand — and sends 12 timed pitches in 20 minutes.

**How it works:**
1. Aditya sets his city, service, and ideal client size.
2. FirstMover scans publicly published business-event signals — press releases, funding feeds, new-business registrations, growth-hiring posts — and matches them to "needs my service now" triggers.
3. Each morning he gets a ranked, deduped list with the trigger, contact route, and recency score so he pitches while the need is fresh.

**Price:** $39/month — one extra $3,000 project a quarter from being first-to-pitch dwarfs the cost.
