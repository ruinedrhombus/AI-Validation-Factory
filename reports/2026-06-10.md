## 💡 IDEA 1 — ScopeGuard
> Watches a solo consultant's client chats and pings them the moment a "quick favour" has quietly turned into unpaid work — before they do it for free.

**Who is this for?**
The agency-of-one: a solo consultant, freelance designer, or fractional marketer juggling 4–6 retainer clients over Slack, email, and a project board.

**What problem does it solve?**
Clients don't ask for big new things — they ask for ten small ones. "Can you also tweak the deck?" "While you're in there, add a second variant." Each feels too tiny to invoice, so the consultant just does it. By month's end they've delivered 30% more than the contract and billed for none of it, and they only notice when they feel burned out and underpaid. There's never a single moment that screams "raise a change order" — that's exactly why it never happens.

**Real world example:**
Meet Aditi. She's a freelance brand strategist with five retainer clients. Every week a client drops three or four "small" extra asks into Slack, and she knocks them out to keep everyone happy. With ScopeGuard, the moment a request drifts past what her signed scope covers, she gets a quiet nudge — "This is the 4th out-of-scope ask from Client X this month, ~6 hrs — draft a change order?" — so she catches the leak in 10 seconds instead of eating it for a month.

**How it works:**
1. She connects the channels she already talks to clients in (Slack / email / Trello / Asana) and pastes each client's scope once.
2. ScopeGuard quietly reads incoming requests and compares them to that scope, tallying what's covered vs. extra.
3. When out-of-scope work crosses a threshold, it alerts her with the receipts and a one-tap "log billable / raise change order."

**Price:** $25/month — it pays for itself the first time it recovers two billable hours a consultant would otherwise have given away.

## 💡 IDEA 2 — MenuCost
> Snaps a photo of this week's supplier invoices and warns a small-kitchen owner the second a dish stops being profitable.

**Who is this for?**
A solo café owner, cloud-kitchen operator, or one-person catering business who sets menu prices once and rarely revisits them.

**What problem does it solve?**
Ingredient prices drift every week — paneer up 12%, oil up 8%, packaging up again — but the menu price stays frozen for months. The owner is "busy running the place" and only discovers a dish has been losing money when the month's books look thin, with no way to know which item bled them. Nobody is recomputing per-dish margin by hand every week; that's the gap.

**Real world example:**
Meet Rohan. He runs a 4-table specialty coffee café and bakes his own pastries. His flour and butter supplier raised prices three times this quarter and he never adjusted his croissant price. With MenuCost, he photographs each delivery invoice; the app reads the line items, maps them to his recipes, and the next morning flags "Croissant margin dropped to 31% — was 48%. Suggested price ₹X." He fixes it in 2 minutes instead of finding out at tax time.

**How it works:**
1. He enters each recipe's ingredients once (or imports them).
2. Every delivery, he photographs the supplier invoice; MenuCost extracts the line-item prices automatically.
3. When any dish's margin falls below his target, it alerts him with the new cost and a suggested reprice.

**Price:** $19/month — one correctly repriced bestseller covers the subscription for a year.
